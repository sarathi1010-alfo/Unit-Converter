## Mental Math for Cooking

Cooking often involves a mix of volume and weight measurements that can vary dramatically between the US customary system and the metric system.

### Cups to Milliliters

One of the most common kitchen conversions is changing cups to milliliters (mL). A standard US cup is equal to roughly 237 mL.

**The Hack:** For quick mental math, you can round this to 240 mL or even 250 mL depending on how precise the recipe needs to be.
- 1/2 cup is about 120 mL.
- 1/4 cup is about 60 mL.
- 1/3 cup is about 80 mL.

By committing these three base numbers (240, 120, 60) to memory, you can easily scale recipes without reaching for a calculator every two minutes. ## Mental Math for Traveling

Traveling across borders introduces entirely new metrics for speed, distance, and temperature. Being able to quickly estimate these values can make your trip much smoother.

### Miles to Kilometers

When driving in a new country, knowing the speed limit is essential for safety and avoiding fines. The conversion factor is 1 mile = 1.609 kilometers.

**The Hack:** Use the Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...). Consecutive numbers in this sequence approximate the conversion.
- 5 miles is roughly 8 kilometers.
- 8 miles is roughly 13 kilometers.
- 13 miles is roughly 21 kilometers.

If the speed limit is 50 miles per hour, you can estimate it as 5 * 10 = 80 kilometers per hour. For exact distances when planning long road trips, use a precise conversion tool to estimate your fuel usage accurately.

## Mental Math for DIY and Home Projects

Whether you're building a bookshelf or buying lumber for a home renovation, converting between imperial and metric lengths is a daily necessity for DIY enthusiasts.

### Inches to Centimeters

The golden rule for length is knowing that 1 inch equals exactly 2.54 centimeters.

**The Hack:** A good rule of thumb is to remember that 10 inches is roughly 25 centimeters (or a quarter of a meter). If you need to convert 20 inches, simply double it to get 50 centimeters.

When you need highly accurate measurements—for instance, when cutting expensive wood or measuring clearance for a new refrigerator—do not rely solely on mental math.
