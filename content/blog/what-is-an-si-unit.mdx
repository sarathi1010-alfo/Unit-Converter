# What is an SI Unit?

**Definition:** An SI Unit (Systeme International d'Unites) is a standard unit of measurement defined by the International System of Units, which is the modern form of the metric system. It provides a universally accepted, coherent set of units used globally in science, industry, and commerce to ensure measurements are consistent everywhere in the world.

## How Do SI Units Work?
The SI system is built upon exactly seven independent "base units" from which all other units (derived units) are created. These base units are defined by fundamental constants of nature (like the speed of light) rather than physical artifacts, ensuring perfect stability over time.

The 7 Base Units are:
- **Meter (m):** Length
- **Kilogram (kg):** Mass
- **Second (s):** Time
- **Ampere (A):** Electric current
- **Kelvin (K):** Thermodynamic temperature
- **Mole (mol):** Amount of substance
- **Candela (cd):** Luminous intensity

## Real-World Example in Scientific Research
If a chemist in Tokyo publishes a paper on chemical reaction rates, they will report temperature in Kelvin and time in seconds. A researcher in Brazil can read this paper and perfectly replicate the experiment without needing to convert any localized measurements, because SI units are the universal language of science.

## SI Base Units vs Derived Units

| Feature | SI Base Units | SI Derived Units |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| **Nature** | Fundamental, independent | Mathematical combinations of base units |
| **Examples** | Meter, Kilogram, Second | Newton (Force), Joule (Energy), Watt (Power) |
