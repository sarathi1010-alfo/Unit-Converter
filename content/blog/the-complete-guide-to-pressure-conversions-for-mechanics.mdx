## Common Pressure Conversions in the Shop

### 1. Bar to PSI (`bar` to `psi`)
This is arguably the most common conversion a mechanic will face. European and Asian vehicles often specify tire pressure or turbo boost pressure in Bars, while North American shops operate almost entirely in PSI.
*   **The Conversion:** 1 Bar = 14.5038 PSI.
*   **Mental Hack:** Multiply by 14.5. For example, a tire requiring 2.5 Bar needs about 36 PSI (2.5 * 14.5 = 36.25).

### 2. Standard Atmosphere to PSI (`atm` to `psi`)
Standard atmosphere (ATM) is often used when discussing baseline pressures or specialized testing equipment.
*   **The Conversion:** 1 ATM = 14.6959 PSI.
*   **Context:** Because 1 ATM is so close to 1 Bar (14.7 vs 14.5 PSI), mechanics sometimes treat them interchangeably in low-pressure scenarios. **Do not do this** in high-pressure hydraulic systems where the 1% difference scales to a dangerous degree.

## Beyond Pressure: Power and Energy

Modern mechanics don't just deal with pneumatics and hydraulics; the rise of EVs and complex electrical systems means understanding power and energy is just as critical.

### 1. Horsepower to Watts (`hp` to `w`)
When comparing traditional internal combustion engines (ICE) to electric motors, you must bridge the gap between mechanical horsepower and electrical watts (or kilowatts).
*   **The Conversion:** 1 Mechanical Horsepower = 745.7 Watts.
*   **Application:** If an EV motor is rated at 150 kW, you divide by 0.7457 to find it produces approximately 201 HP.

### 2. Calories to Kilowatt-Hours (`cal` to `kwh`)
While less common on the shop floor, understanding the energy density of different fuel types or battery systems often requires converting thermal energy (Calories or BTUs) into electrical energy (kWh).
