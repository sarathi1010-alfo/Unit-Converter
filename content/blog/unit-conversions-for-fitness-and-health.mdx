## Body Weight: Pounds, Kilograms, and Stones

The most fundamental metric for many people's fitness journey is body weight. However, depending on where you live or what fitness apps you use, the numbers can be incredibly confusing. The United States relies on pounds (lbs), the vast majority of the world uses kilograms (kg), and the United Kingdom often still refers to stones (st).

### Converting Pounds to Kilograms (and Vice Versa)

The exact conversion factor is 1 kilogram = 2.20462 pounds. When precision is key—such as competing in a weight-class sport like powerlifting or wrestling—you should always use a dedicated [weight conversion calculator](/#categories) to ensure absolute accuracy.

However, for everyday tracking, you can use mental math.

**To convert kilograms to pounds:**
1. Multiply the kg value by 2.
2. Add 10% to that result.

*Example: 80 kg to lbs*
- 80 x 2 = 160
- 10% of 160 = 16
- 160 + 16 = 176 lbs (Actual is 176.37)

**To convert pounds to kilograms:**
1. Divide the lbs value by 2.
2. Subtract 10% from that result.

*Example: 200 lbs to kg*
- 200 / 2 = 100
- 10% of 100 = 10
- 100 - 10 = 90 kg (Actual is 90.71)

### Understanding the British Stone

If you encounter a fitness program from the UK, you might see a weight listed as "12 stone 4". A stone is exactly 14 pounds.
Therefore, "12 stone" is 12 x 14 = 168 pounds. Adding the extra 4 pounds brings the total to 172 lbs. Since the math can get clunky, using a deterministic digital tool is highly recommended for stone conversions.

## Distance and Speed: Kilometers vs. Miles

Whether you're training for a 5K or a marathon, understanding the relationship between miles and kilometers is essential. A common mistake for new runners is misinterpreting pace or distance when using a GPS watch set to unfamiliar units.

### The 5K, 10K, and Marathon

First, memorize these baseline distances, as they form the foundation of global endurance events:
- **5K (5 Kilometers):** 3.1 miles
- **10K (10 Kilometers):** 6.2 miles
- **Half Marathon:** 13.1 miles (21.1 kilometers)
- **Marathon:** 26.2 miles (42.2 kilometers)

### Mental Math for Running

The exact conversion is 1 mile = 1.60934 kilometers. A popular mental hack for runners is using the Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...). The ratio between consecutive numbers approximates the conversion between miles and kilometers perfectly for training purposes.

- 3 miles ≈ 5 kilometers
- 5 miles ≈ 8 kilometers
- 8 miles ≈ 13 kilometers

If your training plan calls for an 8-mile run, but your local trail is marked in kilometers, you instantly know to aim for the 13km marker.

### Treadmill Speed: mph vs km/h

Treadmills in commercial gyms often cause confusion. If you're used to running at 6.0 mph, and you step on a treadmill set to km/h, setting it to 6.0 will feel like a slow walk. To convert mph to km/h, multiply by 1.6.

- 5.0 mph = 8.0 km/h
- 6.0 mph = 9.6 km/h
- 7.0 mph = 11.2 km/h

## Nutrition: Macros, Grams, and Ounces

Nutrition tracking requires the highest level of precision in the fitness world. A few grams off here and there can quickly add up to hundreds of uncounted calories over a week, stalling your fat loss or muscle gain.

### Weighing Your Food: The Case for Grams

Most fitness professionals recommend using a digital kitchen scale and weighing your food in grams, regardless of what country you live in. Why? Because grams are a small, precise unit that eliminates the ambiguity of "cups" or "scoops."

However, if you're buying meat from an American butcher, you will likely purchase it in ounces or pounds, but you need to track it in grams for your macro app.

- **1 Ounce (oz) = 28.35 grams (g)**
- **1 Pound (lb) = 453.6 grams (g)**

### The "Protein Math" Hack

A very common scenario is buying a chicken breast and needing to know the macros. Most tracking apps list the nutritional info per 100 grams. If you know you have an 8-ounce chicken breast:

1. Multiply 8 oz by 28 to find the grams (approx 224g).
2. If chicken breast has roughly 31g of protein per 100g, you multiply 31 by 2.24 to find your total protein (approx 69g).

### Fluids: Milliliters vs. Fluid Ounces

Hydration is just as critical as nutrition. A common goal is drinking a gallon of water a day, but what if your water bottle is marked in milliliters?

- **1 US Fluid Ounce (fl oz) = 29.57 milliliters (mL)**
- **1 US Gallon = 128 fl oz = 3.78 Liters (L)**

If you have a standard 500mL water bottle, you need to drink roughly 7.5 of them to hit a gallon. If you're measuring pre-workout or protein powder mixing ratios, 1 cup is roughly 240mL, or 8 fluid ounces.

## Barbell Math: Navigating the Weight Room

One of the most intimidating places for unit confusion is the weight room. Imagine walking into a new gym, ready to hit a personal record, only to find the plates are in kilograms instead of pounds.

### The "Kilo Plate" Breakdown

In international weightlifting and Olympic lifting, the standard bar is 20kg (44 lbs). The plates are color-coded:
- **Red:** 25kg (55 lbs)
- **Blue:** 20kg (44 lbs)
- **Yellow:** 15kg (33 lbs)
- **Green:** 10kg (22 lbs)

If you're accustomed to American gyms, a "two-plate" bench press refers to two 45lb plates on each side of a 45lb bar, totaling 225 lbs.
If you attempt a "two-plate" lift with blue 20kg plates on a 20kg bar, you are lifting 100kg (220 lbs). It's close, but when attempting maximum lifts, a 5lb difference matters!

### The Multiplier Rule for Lifters

If you're staring at a bar loaded with kg plates and need to know the lb equivalent fast, use the `multiply by 2, add 10%` rule discussed earlier.
A barbell loaded with 140kg:
- 140 x 2 = 280
- 10% of 280 = 28
- 280 + 28 = 308 lbs.
