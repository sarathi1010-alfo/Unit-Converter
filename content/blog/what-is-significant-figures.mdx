# What are Significant Figures?

**Definition:** Significant figures (often called "sig figs") are the specific digits within a number that carry reliable meaning regarding the precision of a measurement. When you measure something, the number of significant figures indicates the exactness of your measuring tool. They prevent us from stating a calculated result with a false, impossible level of precision.

## How Do Significant Figures Work?
When performing math with measurements, the result cannot be more precise than the least precise measurement used in the calculation.

There are strict rules for identifying them:
1. **Non-zero digits** are always significant (e.g., 45 has two).
2. **Zeros between non-zeros** are significant (e.g., 405 has three).
3. **Leading zeros** are never significant (e.g., 0.05 has one).
4. **Trailing zeros** are significant *only* if there is a decimal point (e.g., 5.00 has three, but 500 has one).

## Real-World Example in Laboratory Science
A pharmacist measures exactly 2.50 grams of a compound (three sig figs). If they divide it into three equal doses mathematically, a calculator will show 0.833333... grams. However, writing that full number implies microscopic precision the pharmacist's scale doesn't have. Using sig fig rules, they round the dose to 0.833 grams (three sig figs) to accurately reflect real-world precision.

## Accuracy vs Precision

| Concept | Definition | Example Application |
|---------|------------|---------------------|
| **Accuracy** | How close a measurement is to the true or accepted value. | Hitting the bullseye on a dartboard. |
| **Precision** | How close multiple measurements are to each other, dictated by sig figs. | Hitting the same spot repeatedly, even if it's off-center. |
